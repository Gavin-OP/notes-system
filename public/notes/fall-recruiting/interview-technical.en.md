# Technical Interview

Technical interviews are not limited to software roles. Data, finance, engineering, research, design, and product teams may use coding, cases, system design, specialist knowledge, or portfolio discussion to understand both foundations and reasoning.

## Confirm the format first

Check the job description, invitation, and official guidance for the format, length, tools, and screen-sharing requirements. Ask the recruiter when the scope is unclear; the same job title can lead to very different interviews.

## Make your thinking visible

Restate the goal, clarify assumptions, and explain a basic approach before optimising. While coding or calculating, verbalise important decisions. For a case, separate missing information, analysis, priorities, and trade-offs.

## When you do not know

Explain what you understand, reduce the problem to a small example, start from known facts, and say how you would validate the answer. It is better to be clear about a boundary than to improvise false expertise.

Review every technical claim in your CV, common concepts and edge cases, project trade-offs, and normal, boundary, and failure tests. Finish by asking about the team’s technical challenges, review practices, and support for new joiners.
