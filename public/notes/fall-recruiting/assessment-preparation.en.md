# Preparing for Online Assessments

Online assessments, video interviews, and personality questionnaires are common pre-interview steps, each measuring distinct skills or traits. Rather than broadly practicing all 'test banks,' a targeted approach—identifying specific assessment types and reviewing official guidance—is more effective and less draining.

## Understanding the Assessment Types

Before you start practicing, understand the specific assessment you've been invited to take. Each type evaluates different skills or traits:

- **Numerical Reasoning:** Interpreting tables, charts, ratios, and business data.
- **Verbal Reasoning:** Evaluating statements based *only* on provided text, not general knowledge or external information.
- **Logical / Diagrammatic Reasoning:** Identifying rules, relationships, and sequences, often presented through abstract patterns.
- **Game-based Assessments:** Evaluating cognitive abilities, behavioral preferences, or work styles through game-like tasks.
- **Situational Judgement Test (SJT):** Choosing the most appropriate response or course of action in simulated work-related scenarios.
- **Personality / Work Style Questionnaire:** Understanding your preferences, behavioral tendencies, and adaptability in a work environment.
- **Role-Specific Skills Test:** Assessing practical abilities required for a particular role, such as SQL, coding, Excel, language proficiency, writing, or case studies.
- **Asynchronous Video Interview:** Recording your verbal responses to prompts within limited preparation and answer times.

Providers like Aon categorize recruitment tests into cognitive ability, focus and multitasking, language or coding skills, situational judgment, and work style. Companies often customize content, so your invitation email, the official Candidate Guide, and any provided practice questions are your most valuable resources.

## Ability Tests: Familiarity Over Guessing

For reasoning tests, familiarity with the format and interface outweighs trying to guess specific questions. Use the official sample questions from the vendor to familiarize yourself with the timer, navigation, calculator rules, and whether you can revisit previous questions.

Scores are often interpreted against a comparison group; there is no universal “pass score.” The employer—not the test vendor—usually determines progression.

After practicing, analyze your errors: Were they due to unfamiliar knowledge, misreading, calculation errors, or poor time management?

For numerical questions, focus on understanding units, baselines, and ratio relationships. For verbal questions, base your judgment strictly on the provided text, avoiding external information. For logical questions, describe the underlying rule, then verify it against the options. If you get stuck during the actual test, decide whether to skip it temporarily based on the platform's rules, to avoid getting stuck on one question.

Aon's [Practice Tasks](https://assessment.aon.com/aon.assessment/media/files/factsheets/Practice-Tasks_ix.pdf) include examples, answers, and explanations, which can help you understand the structure and problem-solving approach of such official practice materials.

## Personality & Situational Judgement Tests: Reflecting Your Authentic Work Style

Personality questionnaires don't have a single 'perfect' profile for every role. Answer honestly and consistently; this is more natural and truly reflective of you than trying to guess what the company wants to see. If two descriptions sound like you, consider which behavior you exhibit more frequently in a learning or work context.

For situational judgment questions, consider factors like safety and compliance, impact on customers and team, whether to gather facts first, and communicating at the appropriate level. Always refer to specific role responsibilities and company guidelines for the most relevant standards.

## Asynchronous Video Interviews: A Time-Limited Expression

Before you begin recording, test your camera, microphone, internet connection, lighting, and background. Confirm if re-recording is allowed, providing a second chance if needed. When answering, start with your core conclusion, then elaborate with reasons and examples. Looking near the camera lens appears more natural than watching your own image on screen.

Outline keywords and a story structure for preparation; memorizing full scripts can increase anxiety if you forget a line. Practice by recording short answers on your phone, then review your voice, pace, and whether you truly addressed the question.

## Before You Start: Optimizing Your Test Environment

Double-check the assessment's time zone, deadline, recommended browser, required ID, scratch paper, and calculator rules. Close unnecessary notifications, ensure your device is fully charged, and allow ample buffer time for login or system checks. If you require accessibility accommodations or reasonable adjustments (e.g., extended test time), apply as early as possible through the recruiter and assessment platform's process.

After completing the assessment, note the platform, question types, your general feeling, and any parts where you got stuck. This information will be more useful than starting a fresh search when similar assessments appear again.
