# Job Boards

Job boards are powerful tools for quickly understanding the market, discovering different job titles, and keeping opportunities flowing through filters and alerts. But remember, information update speeds vary. Think of them as an entry point, not the sole source of truth.

## Common Job Boards in Mainland China

-   [BOSS Zhipin](https://www.zhipin.com/): Covers a wide range of industries, includes sections for campus recruitment and overseas returnees, and is suitable for cross-searching by city, position, and company. Its direct chat feature allows for quick understanding of job details.
-   [51job](https://www.51job.com/) and [Zhaopin](https://www.zhaopin.com/): Comprehensive recruitment platforms with a large volume of information. They can be used to observe job titles and recruitment trends across different regions and industries.
-   [Shixiseng](https://www.shixiseng.com/): Focuses on internship opportunities, suitable for students looking to gain their first or next internship experience.
-   [Nowcoder Jobs](https://www.nowcoder.com/jobs/): Concentrates on campus recruitment and technical positions, often linked with written tests and interview discussions, making it an important resource for technical students.
-   **Government and Public Service (Official Positions):**
    *   [National College Graduates Employment Service Platform](https://www.ncss.cn/): Managed by the Ministry of Education, it provides employment policies, job information, and recruitment activities for graduates.
    *   [National Public Employment Service Platform](https://www.12333.gov.cn/job/): Managed by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, it provides nationwide employment services.
    *   [China Public Recruitment Network](https://job.mohrss.gov.cn/): Also an official platform, gathering various recruitment information.

## Common Job Boards in Hong Kong

-   [Jobsdb Hong Kong](https://hk.jobsdb.com/): One of Hong Kong's largest job boards, offering a wide range of job categories, saved searches, and application tracking features.
-   [CTgoodjobs](https://www.ctgoodjobs.hk/) and [cpjobs](https://www.cpjobs.com/hk/en): Offer more positions from local Hong Kong companies, industry classifications, and career information, providing more localized options.
-   [Interactive Employment Service of the Labour Department, Hong Kong](https://www2.jobs.gov.hk/0/tc/information/ourservices/findjobs/): A comprehensive employment service platform provided by the Hong Kong Labour Department, covering positions across various industries.
-   [Hong Kong Civil Service Bureau Recruitment](https://www.csb.gov.hk/english/recruit/7.html): Government positions are based on this official information, which is the sole official entry point for applying to the civil service.
-   **University and Alumni Networks:** Many universities' Career Portals, school emails, and graduate recruitment events offer opportunities exclusively for students or alumni, which usually have less competition and are worth checking alongside public platforms.

No single platform can cover all market opportunities, and not all positions are equally suitable for students. Employers choose different channels, and the same position might appear on multiple platforms simultaneously.

## Search Flexibly, Use Alerts Wisely

Don't just search for direct job titles. Try combining keywords for job titles, skills, industries, and locations. For example, if you're looking for a "Data Analyst" internship, you could try different terms like "Data Analytics Intern," "Business Intelligence Analyst," or "BI intern."
Also, try synonymous job titles, as different companies might use different names for the same function.
Once you find relevant search criteria, be sure to save them and set up email or app alerts. New opportunities will be delivered automatically, saving you the effort of daily refreshing.

## Verify Information Before Acting

Job board information update speeds vary, and you might encounter old positions, third-party reposts, or duplicate postings. Therefore, after finding a suitable position, **always go back to the company's official website recruitment page to verify the information**.
On the official website, confirm the following key information:
*   **Job Description**: Whether it matches the platform's description and if there are more detailed explanations.
*   **Location**: The specific work location, and whether remote or off-site work is supported.
*   **Target Candidate**: Clearly states whether it's for fresh graduates, interns, or experienced professionals.
*   **Application Deadline**: This is one of the most important pieces of information; ensure you submit your application before the deadline.
*   **Application Method**: Confirm the correct application portal and process.
*   **Job ID**: Some companies assign a unique ID to each position for easier tracking.

If there's any inconsistency between the official website and the job board information, **always refer to the company's official recruitment page and direct communication from the recruiter**. This can help you avoid wasting time on closed or incorrect listings and prevent encountering scams.

## Beware of Recruitment Scams

While job boards do their best to vet listings, scam information can still slip through. Here are some common signs of recruitment scams:
*   **Requests for Payment**: Any recruitment asking for "training fees," "deposits," "uniform fees," or "background check fees" is likely a scam. Legitimate companies do not charge candidates during the recruitment process.
*   **Mismatched Information**: The recruiter's email domain doesn't match the company's official website, or the contact person's information cannot be found on the company's official website.
*   **Undue Haste**: Rushing to extend an offer or demanding immediate onboarding without sufficient interviews and background checks.
*   **Moving Off-Platform**: Asking to immediately switch communication to private chat tools like WeChat or QQ, instead of using the platform or the company's official email.
*   **Requesting Sensitive Information**: Asking for highly sensitive personal information like ID numbers, bank card numbers, or passwords during the initial contact stage.
*   **Vague Job Details**: Being unclear about job responsibilities, salary, company information, or promising "high pay for easy work."

If you encounter suspicious situations, stop communicating immediately and report them.

## Build Your Application Tracker

To manage your applications efficiently, create a simple tracker (this could be a spreadsheet, Notion, or any tool you prefer). Each time you find an interesting position, record the following:
*   **Company Name**
*   **Job Title**
*   **Job Link** (preferably to the company's official website page)
*   **Date Found**
*   **Application Deadline**
*   **Application Status** (e.g., Not Applied, Applied, Interviewing, Rejected, Offer)
*   **Notes** (e.g., 'Resume needs revision', 'Contacted referrer')

Job boards help you discover entry points, but the final judgment and action still need to come back to the actual job description, company information, and your personal career direction.
