# Job Boards

Job boards are powerful tools for quickly surveying the market, discovering various role titles, and maintaining a steady flow of opportunities with saved searches and alerts. But remember, update speeds vary. Treat each listing as an entry point, not the sole source of truth.

## Common Mainland China entry points

-   [BOSS Zhipin](https://www.zhipin.com/) covers a broad range of roles, including campus and overseas-graduate positions. Its direct chat function allows for quick inquiries.
-   [51job](https://www.51job.com/) and [Zhaopin](https://www.zhaopin.com/) are general recruitment platforms with extensive listings. Use them to observe role titles and hiring trends across regions and industries.
-   [Shixiseng](https://www.shixiseng.com/) focuses on internships, ideal for students seeking their first or next work experience.
-   [Nowcoder Jobs](https://www.nowcoder.com/jobs/) concentrates on campus and technology recruitment. It's a key resource for tech students, often linked with assessment and interview communities.
-   **Government and Public Service Channels (for official roles):**
    *   [China National College Student Employment Service](https://www.ncss.cn/): Managed by the Ministry of Education, offering graduate employment policies, job information, and recruitment events.
    *   [China National Employment Public Service Platform](https://www.12333.gov.cn/job/): Managed by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, providing nationwide employment services.
    *   [China Public Recruitment Network](https://job.mohrss.gov.cn/): Another official platform aggregating various recruitment notices.

## Common Hong Kong entry points

-   [Jobsdb Hong Kong](https://hk.jobsdb.com/) is one of Hong Kong's largest recruitment platforms, offering broad role categories, saved searches, and application tracking.
-   [CTgoodjobs](https://www.ctgoodjobs.hk/) and [cpjobs](https://www.cpjobs.com/hk/en) provide additional Hong Kong-specific listings and career resources, offering more localized options.
-   [Hong Kong Labour Department Interactive Employment Service](https://www2.jobs.gov.hk/0/en/information/ourservices/findjobs/): A comprehensive employment service platform from the Hong Kong Labour Department, covering various industry roles.
-   The [Hong Kong Civil Service Bureau recruitment page](https://www.csb.gov.hk/english/recruit/7.html) is the official entry point for civil-service roles.
-   **University and Alumni Networks:** Many university career portals, student emails, and graduate recruitment events offer student- or alumni-only opportunities. These often face less competition and are worth checking alongside public platforms.

No single board lists every market opportunity, nor is every role on a platform equally suitable. Employers choose different channels, and the same opening may appear on several platforms.

## Search flexibly and use alerts

Don't just search for direct role titles. Combine keywords for roles, skills, industries, and locations. For example, if you're looking for a "Data Analyst" internship, you might try "Data Analytics Intern," "Business Intelligence Analyst," or "BI intern." Also, experiment with synonymous job titles; companies often use different names for the same function. Once you find relevant search criteria, save them and set up email or app alerts. New opportunities will be delivered automatically, saving you daily search effort.

## Verify before investing time

Job board information can be outdated, reposted by third parties, or duplicated. After finding a promising role, **always verify the details on the company's official career page.**
On the official site, confirm these key pieces of information:
*   **Responsibilities:** Check if they match the board's description and if there are more detailed explanations.
*   **Location:** Specific work location, and whether remote or hybrid options are available.
*   **Candidate Eligibility:** Clearly stated requirements for fresh graduates, interns, or experienced professionals.
*   **Application Deadline:** This is one of the most crucial details; ensure you apply before it.
*   **Application Method:** Confirm the correct application portal and process.
*   **Role ID:** Some companies assign a unique ID to each role for tracking.

If there's a discrepancy, **always defer to the company's official career page and direct communication from the recruiter.** This saves time on closed or inaccurate listings and protects you from scams.

## Beware of recruitment scams

While job boards vet listings, scams can still appear. Here are common red flags:
*   **Requests for Payment:** Any request for 'training fees,' 'deposits,' 'uniform costs,' or 'background check fees' is likely a scam. Legitimate companies don't charge candidates during recruitment.
*   **Mismatched Information:** Recruiter's email domain doesn't match the company's official website, or the contact person isn't found on official channels.
*   **Undue Haste:** Rushed offers or immediate start dates without sufficient interviews or background checks.
*   **Moving Off-Platform:** Requests to switch immediately to private messaging apps (e.g., WeChat, QQ) instead of the platform or official company email.
*   **Demands for Sensitive Information:** Requests for highly sensitive personal data (e.g., ID numbers, bank account details, passwords) during initial contact.
*   **Vague Job Details:** Unclear descriptions of responsibilities, salary, company information, or promises of 'high pay for easy work.'

If you encounter suspicious situations, stop communicating and report the listing immediately.

## Build your application tracker

To manage applications efficiently, create a simple tracker (a spreadsheet, Notion, or any preferred tool). For each interesting role, record:
*   **Company Name**
*   **Role Title**
*   **Role Link** (preferably the company's official career page)
*   **Discovery Date**
*   **Application Deadline**
*   **Application Status** (e.g., Not Applied, Applied, Interviewing, Rejected, Offer)
*   **Notes** (e.g., 'Resume needs tailoring,' 'Contacted referrer')

Job boards help you find entry points, but your final judgment and action should always align with the actual job description, company information, and your career direction.
