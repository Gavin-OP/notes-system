# Portfolio and Personal Site

If you have things you want to showcase but can't fit on your resume, you can include them in a portfolio or on a personal site. A portfolio is for delving into your work, while a personal site organizes your self-introduction, experiences, and various links into one entry point.

## Choose Truly Relevant Projects

More work isn't always better. Prioritize projects that are relevant to your target role and demonstrate your individual contributions, helping readers understand:

- What was the problem, and why was it worth solving?
- What was your role and what were the constraints?
- How did you analyze, design, or implement it?
- What were the results, and what did you learn?

## Help Recruiters Understand Quickly

Start with conclusions and key achievements, then provide details. Screenshots, charts, and links with explanations will make it easier to read; for team projects, clearly explain your specific contributions. When dealing with company or user data, remember to anonymize it first and confirm that the content is suitable for public display.

## Form Follows Content

A portfolio can be a PDF, Notion page, or GitHub repository, while a personal site can be a simple website. The form should serve the content: stable loading, clear structure, and mobile readability are already professional enough.
