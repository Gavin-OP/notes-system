# Offer Decision and Job Search Review

Reaching the offer stage can be exciting, but it might also bring a new kind of anxiety: which option is better, should you wait, or what would others choose? When you lay out the information alongside your own priorities, the decision usually becomes clearer over time.

## "Fit" Is Actually Discoverable

Companies assess whether your skills, motivation, and work style suit the role; you, in turn, observe whether the actual work, team, and development direction suit you. This has always been a mutual selection process.

Genuine interest doesn't automatically translate into an offer, but it does influence the depth of your research, the naturalness of your motivation, and your communication style. After rounds of interviews, you've also gathered a lot of information about the team and yourself.

## First, Verify What the Offer Truly Includes

Review the written documents and confirm: position and team, work location, start date, probation period, base salary, bonus rules, benefits, holidays, working hours, rotation or training, offer acceptance deadline, and any attached conditions. If there are terms you don't understand, you can politely ask HR; for important information, try to get written confirmation.

Record verbal promises, employee experiences, and contract terms separately. Also, understand salary figures in the context of taxes, cost of living, commute, and actual working hours, rather than just comparing a maximum value.

## Also, Understand Labor Rights and Protections

When reviewing a contract for the first time, it's easy to get confused by terms like probation period, bonuses, insurance, provident funds, or MPF. Here's a navigation guide to official resources, which does not explain specific cases for you or constitute legal advice. Amounts, percentages, eligibility, and local implementation may be subject to change, so please refer to the latest official pages for your work location.

### Mainland China

- [Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security: "Labor Law of the People's Republic of China"](https://www.mohrss.gov.cn/xxgk2020/fdzdgknr/zcfg/fl/202011/t20201102_394625.html): You can learn about the basic framework of labor contracts, working hours, rest and holidays, wages, and labor protection.
- ["Social Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China"](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_e81d115419b4463ebb59ec46467fb136.html): Introduces the basic old-age, medical, work-related injury, unemployment, and maternity insurance systems.
- [Ministry of Justice: "Regulations on the Management of Housing Provident Funds"](https://xzfy.moj.gov.cn/c/2021-01-17/488809.shtml): Explains the framework for housing provident fund contributions, ownership, withdrawal, and use.

When you receive an offer, you can continue to confirm with HR the official labor contract signing time, probation period, social insurance and provident fund contribution location and base, bonus conditions, and overtime and leave systems. For specific disputes, you can consult the local human resources and social security department or public legal service channels in your work location.

### Hong Kong

- [Hong Kong Labour Department: "A Concise Guide to the Employment Ordinance"](https://www.labour.gov.hk/eng/public/ConciseGuide.htm): Covers topics such as employment contracts, wages, rest days, holidays, sickness allowance, termination of employment, and employment protection.
- [Hong Kong MPFA: MPF Information for Employees](https://www.mpfa.org.hk/en/role-based/employees): Introduces employee participation in MPF, contributions, account management, and handling after leaving employment.

The applicability may differ for interns, part-time workers, those on short-term contracts, and individuals holding work visas. If you encounter uncertain terms, first save the written documents, then inquire with the Hong Kong Labour Department, MPFA, or qualified professionals.

## Supplement Team Information from the Interview Process

Recall how different interviewers described the work, challenges, expectations for new hires, and success metrics: were their answers specific, did team members understand each other's work, and would you be willing to continue talking with these people? The interview process experience isn't the entire reality of the job, but it's some of the few pieces of information you've observed firsthand.

If you still have key questions, you can request another conversation with your future manager or team members. Focusing your questions on the role itself will likely yield more useful answers than broadly asking "What's the company culture like?"

## When You Need More Time or Communication

After receiving an offer, first express your gratitude and confirm the deadline. If you genuinely have courses, other processes, or important information to handle, you can ask early and honestly if there's reasonable flexibility. When discussing terms, state the items you wish to discuss and your reasoning, maintaining a professional tone.

When accepting, declining, or withdrawing from other processes, you can be concise and polite. Keep a record, confirm that the other party has received your message, and preserve respect for future professional relationships.

The funnel simply helps pinpoint where feedback appears:

- If you've considered very few roles seriously, you might need to expand your keywords or company pool;
- If few applications proceed to assessments, you could review your role fit and application materials;
- If many candidates drop off after assessments, you might review the question types and your time management strategy;
- If many candidates drop off after interviews, combine this with an interview review to look for recurring signals;
- If the process went well but you don't want to accept, perhaps your direction and environment assessment need updating.

When the sample size is small, there's no need to rush to conclusions, as hiring is also influenced by many external factors like available spots, timing, and competition. Numbers help you ask questions; they don't judge you.

## Take What You've Gained from This Round

The number of offers is just one part of this experience. Gaining clarity on what you want to do, expressing yourself more naturally, knowing how to research companies, and being able to identify mismatched opportunities are all skills that will stay with you.

Receiving a suitable offer is important; gradually seeing your own abilities, preferences, and boundaries is equally valuable. Regardless of the outcome, this round of real-world feedback can become a more confident starting point for your next phase.
