# Social Platforms: From Experience Posts to Reliable Judgement

Social platforms can reveal what official pages rarely describe: the daily work behind a title, how a recruitment stage felt, team culture, and how someone with a similar background made a decision. Treat them as a library of experiences, not an official database.

## What different platforms can reveal

- **LinkedIn:** career histories, transitions, company updates, public jobs, and industry discussion. Profiles can suggest common backgrounds, but they are curated and incomplete samples.
- **Glassdoor:** anonymous company reviews, interview experiences, salary contributions, and job listings. Look for recurring patterns rather than treating one extreme review as the whole company.
- **Xiaohongshu:** student and graduate timelines, process notes, city life, role experiences, and emotional reality. Posts can be timely and concrete, but may omit context, include commercial promotion, or turn one person's experience into a universal rule.
- **Industry communities, forums, and group chats:** useful for discovering terminology and recent discussion. The longer the chain of retelling, the more important it is to find the original source.

## Verify information in three layers

1. **Separate facts, experiences, and advice.** A deadline is a verifiable fact; “the team moves quickly” is an experience; “everyone should prepare this way” is advice.
2. **Check date and context.** Which year, region, business line, and candidate level does the post describe? Compare several independent accounts and note what repeats.
3. **Return to a primary source.** Confirm openings, eligibility, responsibilities, location, and deadlines through the employer careers site, current JD, or direct recruiter communication. Use government or regulator sources for law, work eligibility, and employment conditions.

## Turn information into a decision

For a useful post, record only four things: the new information, the context it applies to, what remains unverified, and where you will check next. That produces decision material instead of an anxiety-inducing screenshot collection.

The strongest use of social media is to improve your questions. If several posts say a role involves substantial client communication, check the JD and ask about the real proportion during a coffee chat. Information becomes part of your Path after discovery, verification, questioning, and judgement.
