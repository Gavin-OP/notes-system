# Stress Interview

A stress interview may use rapid follow-ups, challenged assumptions, time pressure, or a deliberately serious tone to observe communication under uncertainty. Pressure is not the same as disrespect.

Slow down and identify whether the challenge concerns your evidence, logic, or choice. Restate the concern and add the missing reasoning. If your first answer has a gap, correcting it is often stronger than defending it indefinitely.

You can ask for a moment to organise your thoughts. When interrupted, check whether the interviewer wants a shorter answer or a different direction.

Questions about work and reasoning are different from humiliation about identity, appearance, family plans, or personality. You may ask how a question relates to the role, decline private details while confirming work requirements, request another wording, or end the interview if boundaries continue to be crossed.

Afterwards, record the question, time, participants, and response. The process is also evidence about the team you are considering.
