# Understanding Roles and the Market

A job title is only a label. The useful questions are what the team actually owns, what problems the role solves, and what evidence the employer expects from a candidate.

## Read several JDs together

Compare responsibilities, required skills, preferred experience, language expectations, and team context. Repeated phrases usually reveal the role's core; unusual requirements may belong to one company rather than the whole profession.

Sort each requirement into three groups: evidence you already have, a gap you can strengthen, and something that is not worth prioritising now. Evidence may come from coursework, internships, projects, societies, volunteering, or part-time work.

Look beyond tools. JDs often signal soft skills such as leadership, communication, attention to detail, judgement, ownership, and collaboration. Translate each one into a situation where you demonstrated it. You do not need to match every line before applying; the comparison simply helps you decide where your time is most valuable.
