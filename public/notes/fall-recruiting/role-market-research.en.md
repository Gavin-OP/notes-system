# Understanding Roles and the Market

Job titles can feel like a forest of abbreviations. A market map makes them easier to read: how an industry creates value, where a company sits, and what problem a role solves inside that system.

## Start with the industry's value chain

Look at the customers, revenue model, partners, regulators, and the technology or policy changes shaping the industry. You do not need to produce an analyst report. Being able to explain how the business works in your own words is already useful.

## Understand the company's position

Size, maturity, and business model can change the daily work behind the same title. Established organisations may offer clearer divisions of responsibility and training; smaller teams may offer broader scope. Neither is automatically better—the question is which learning environment suits you.

## See which roles actually exist

Market research is also about discovering the full set of options. Knowing only one fashionable title can funnel all your preparation into a narrow route before you have seen the neighbouring paths.

Quant is a useful example. “I want to work in quant” can point towards quantitative research, quantitative trading, quantitative development, risk modelling, model validation, or data- and strategy-related roles. Titles and boundaries vary by employer, as do the emphasis on mathematics, programming, markets, and communication. A business student who has heard of only one route may prepare very hard while missing another role that fits better.

Build the map from organisation type to team, role family, day-to-day problem, and the evidence expected from candidates. Then validate it against real roles on employer career sites. The goal is not to memorise every title; it is to understand how the market divides its work and where else you might look.

## Compare role families, not only titles

Break a role into the people or things it works with, its main activities, its outputs, and its working environment. [O*NET occupation reports](https://www.onetonline.org/help/online/summary) use a similar structure—Tasks, Technology Skills, Work Activities, Work Context, Skills, and Knowledge—which can help compare roles even outside the United States.

## Look at progression and transferable skills

Consider what domain knowledge, tools, and judgement the role builds; common next steps; skills that transfer across industries; and whether an early-career hire can receive feedback and own meaningful work. A first role does not decide an entire life. A clearer view of transferable skills keeps more routes open.
