# Handling Unusual Interview Situations

Some interview questions are not in a practice bank: the interviewer is late, the link fails, the format changes, or a question crosses a boundary. Knowing your options makes it easier to think clearly.

## A late interviewer or technical problem

Check the time zone, link, and recent email. After a reasonable wait, tell the recruiter that you are present and ask for an update. If the delay affects another commitment, state your end time and discuss rescheduling. For technical problems, try the basic recovery steps, leave a written contact route, and request a backup link or new time.

## A changed format

If a one-to-one becomes a panel, an online meeting becomes in-person, or a test is added, confirm the length, participants, and requirements. Ask for an adjustment when the change affects travel, classes, or accessibility needs.

## Disrespectful or irrelevant questions

Ask how the question relates to the role, decline private information while confirming legitimate work arrangements, or request another wording. You may pause or end an interview that continues to cross boundaries. Do not disclose confidential information or invent work; use anonymised context, methods, and your own contribution instead.

Record facts, timing, participants, and exact wording afterwards. You can contact recruitment, speak with someone you trust, or consult official local employment-rights channels. An interview helps you assess the organisation too.
