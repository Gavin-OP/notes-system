# How to Start Your First Internship

The hardest part of a first internship is often the lack of an existing internship to prove what you can do. Coursework, societies, part-time work, volunteering, and personal projects can all provide evidence when you explain the problem, your contribution, and the outcome clearly.

## Look for work that welcomes beginners

Prioritise concrete responsibilities, access to guidance, and exposure to real work. Alongside formal programmes, consider university labs, start-ups, non-profits, faculty or alumni projects, and well-scoped short assignments.

## Replace “no experience” with evidence

Match recurring JD requirements with things you have already done. A thoughtful course project, a useful analysis, or a real collaboration can make your starting point visible.

## Decide whether the experience is worthwhile

Clarify the work, supervision, time, compensation, and basic protections. A first internship need not be perfect, but it should teach you something about the work and leave you with a skill, artefact, or story you can use again.
