# Resume Writing and Experience Stories

A useful resume lets a reader answer three questions quickly: what you worked on, what you contributed, and what changed because of it.

Lead bullets with the work or outcome, then add the method and context. Prefer concrete verbs and explain numbers rather than decorating every line with metrics. Team results are valuable, but make your own contribution clear enough to discuss in an interview.

Different roles may justify different resume versions. An analysis role may foreground data and decisions; a product role may foreground user needs and delivery; a finance role may foreground judgement and risk. The facts stay the same while the order and emphasis change.

Keep formatting consistent, make links work, and export a readable PDF. A final review for dates, tense, spacing, and unexplained jargon often improves the document more than another round of dramatic rewriting.
