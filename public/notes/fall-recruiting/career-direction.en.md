# Clarifying Your Direction and Realistic Constraints

It's completely normal to have only a vague direction when you're just starting out. Career choices are rarely figured out all at once; they usually become clearer as you read JDs, have conversations, apply, and interview.

## Start with the Job Content

Job titles can be an entry point, but what's truly worth looking at is the actual job content. When browsing a few real JDs, you can pay attention to:

- What problems does this role typically solve?
- Which tasks appear repeatedly?
- Do these ways of working align with my interests and personality?

Roles with the same title can be completely different at different companies, and roles with different titles might involve similar tasks. Highlighting recurring tasks is more useful than just remembering job titles.

## Validate Your Direction with Experience

Look at your courses, projects, internships, club activities, and part-time jobs together. Which experiences did you feel deeply engaged in? Which skills do you already have evidence for? What qualities do others frequently recognize in you? These clues can tell you what "fit" might look like.

Your direction doesn't need to be a perfect match. Being able to point to a real experience that shows where your interest comes from and how you performed in it is already more concrete than abstractly saying, "I really like it."

## Set Realistic Boundaries

You can also consider the learning opportunities, development path, and transferability of skills for a role, then combine that with the hiring timeline, location arrangements, and preparation costs. Roles that you have low interest in and require a lot of extra preparation can easily sneakily take over an entire evening of overtime; understanding these realistic conditions helps you protect your energy.

Your direction will continue to adjust as you apply and interview. At this moment, you don't need to answer "What do I want to do for the rest of my life?" Just find a path you're willing to explore first and have an opportunity to enter.
