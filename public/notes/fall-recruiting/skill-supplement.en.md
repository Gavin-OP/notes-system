# Skill Supplement: Choose Your Skill Set Based on the Role

LeetCode, investment banking interview questions, competitions, data analysis tools, financial certifications... preparing these skills is a side quest on your job search journey. Some of them require long-term accumulation, and the learning and training process might start years before your official job search and continue even after you've landed a job. Each skill requires time and effort, so instead of trying to learn as much as possible, it's more important to decide based on your target role: which skills you truly need to supplement during your job search, and which ones are worth prioritizing.

## First, Identify What Kind of Evidence You Need

| Type | What problems it often solves | What you can leave behind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role Screening & Interview Question Banks | Addressing specific written tests or interview assessments | Problem-solving records, interview performance |
| Tools & Projects | Demonstrating ability to solve problems with common tools | GitHub projects, analysis reports |
| Competitions | Supplementing collaboration and project evidence when internship experience is limited | Works, rankings, team stories |
| Professional Qualifications | Systematically learning a professional field, or meeting specific career path requirements | Exam progress, formal qualifications |

Start by looking for signals in the JD of your target role, descriptions of written test/interview processes, and real feedback from job seekers. If you see the same requirement repeatedly, then decide whether to invest in it. If you start a skill-supplementing side quest just because "everyone else is learning it," the most likely skill you'll gain is having many web pages open at once.

## Role Screening & Interview Question Banks

- **LeetCode:** Suitable for roles with a clear process that tests algorithms, data structures, coding, or SQL. Whether and to what extent you need to practice algorithm problems is still determined by your target role and the interview process. Common problem sets include:
  - [LeetCode 75](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/leetcode-75/): 75 high-frequency fundamental problems, suitable for building a framework of common algorithm problem types in 1–3 months.
  - [Top Interview 150](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-interview-150/): More comprehensive coverage, suitable for systematic technical interview preparation.
  - [Top 100 Liked](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-100-liked/): 100 highly-rated problems in the LeetCode community, suitable for supplementing common interview problem types.
  - [Blind 75](https://neetcode.io/practice/practice/blind75): Classic 75 algorithm interview problems, suitable for quickly covering core problem types.
  - [NeetCode 150](https://neetcode.io/practice/practice/neetcode150): Expands on Blind 75 to 150 problems, categorized by topics such as Arrays, Trees, Graphs, Dynamic Programming, suitable for systematic practice.
  - [SQL 50](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-sql-50/): 50 basic to medium difficulty SQL problems, suitable for roles like Data Analyst, BI, Data Science, etc., that test SQL.
  - [Advanced SQL 50](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/premium-sql-50/): Covers high-frequency problems from basic to advanced SQL, suitable for those needing more in-depth SQL interview preparation.
  - [30 Days of Pandas](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/30-days-of-pandas/): Focuses on common Pandas interview content, suitable for data analysis and some data science roles.
  - [LeetCode Study Plans](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/): The entry point for all official LeetCode study plans, where you can choose corresponding problem sets based on your target role and skills.

- **Investment Banking Interview Questions, 400 Questions, Red Book, and other interview materials:** These are more like knowledge question banks for specific recruitment processes such as Investment Banking and Private Equity, usually focusing on Accounting, Valuation, DCF, M&A, LBO, Technical Questions, and Behavioral Questions. The focus of assessment varies significantly across different roles, companies, and recruitment stages, so it's a good idea to first confirm the interview process for your target role before choosing the relevant materials. Common official or publicly available resources include:
  - [Breaking Into Wall Street – Investment Banking Interview Guide / 400 Questions](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/investment-banking-interview-guide/): Classic Investment Banking interview preparation materials. The official Interview Guide includes their 400 Questions Guide, covering common Technical and Behavioral Questions.
  - [Wall Street Prep – Investment Banking Interview Guide / The Red Book](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/self-study-programs/the-red-book/): Wall Street Prep's official interview question bank, containing a large number of Investment Banking Technical and Behavioral Questions, suitable for more systematic preparation for investment banking interviews.
  - [Wall Street Prep – Investment Banking Interview Questions](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/top-10-investment-banking-interview-questions/): Free compilation of common investment banking interview questions, covering core Technical Questions like Valuation, DCF, and Accounting.
  - [Wall Street Prep – Investment Banking Interview Guide](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/investment-banking-interview/): Introduces the overall Investment Banking Interview process and outlines the main preparation directions for Behavioral and Technical Questions.
  - [Wall Street Prep – M&A Interview Questions](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/ma-interview-questions/): Focuses on preparing for Merger Model, Accretion / Dilution, and M&A-related questions.
  - [Wall Street Prep – Private Equity Interview Questions](https://www.wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/25-private-equity-interview-questions-you-must-know/): Suitable for students further applying to Private Equity, covering PE Technical Questions and common interview topics.
  - [Breaking Into Wall Street – M&A Interview Questions](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/ma-and-merger-models/ma-interview-questions/): Official free tutorial, focusing on Merger Model and common questions in M&A interviews.
  - [Breaking Into Wall Street – LBO Interview Questions](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/leveraged-buyouts-and-lbo-models/lbo-interview-questions/): Suitable for preparing for LBO, Private Equity, and some Investment Banking Technical Interviews.
  - [Breaking Into Wall Street – Equity Value & Enterprise Value Interview Questions](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/equity-value-enterprise-value/equity-value-and-enterprise-value-interview-questions-what-to-expect/): Specialized practice for Equity Value, Enterprise Value, and related Valuation Questions.
  - [Breaking Into Wall Street – Behavioral Interview Questions](https://breakingintowallstreet.com/kb/interviews/investment-banking-behavioral-questions/): Focuses on preparing for Behavioral Questions such as Why Investment Banking, Walk Me Through Your Resume, and team experiences.
  - [CFI – Investment Banking Interview Questions](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/career/real-investment-banking-interview-questions-form/): CFI's official compilation of investment banking interview questions, which can be used to supplement Accounting, Valuation, and scenario-based questions.
  - [Macabacus – Learn Finance](https://macabacus.com/learn): Provides public learning materials for Operating Model, M&A Model, LBO Model, etc., more suitable for understanding the Financial Modeling knowledge behind interview questions rather than just memorizing answers.

## Data, Machine Learning, and Project Tools

- **Pandas / NumPy:** Among the most commonly used Python tools for data analysis and scientific computing. Pandas is primarily used for reading, cleaning, transforming, and analyzing tabular data, while NumPy provides array operations and extensive numerical computing functions. It's suitable for practicing with real data problems, such as cleaning a CSV file, calculating business metrics, handling missing values, or performing an Exploratory Data Analysis. Common learning resources include:
  - [pandas Getting Started](https://pandas.pydata.org/docs/getting_started/index.html): Pandas official getting started documentation, suitable for a first systematic understanding of core operations like Series, DataFrame, data reading, filtering, merging, and statistics.
  - [pandas User Guide](https://pandas.pydata.org/docs/user_guide/index.html): More complete official documentation, which can serve as a reference manual in actual projects.
  - [NumPy Learn](https://numpy.org/learn/): NumPy's official curated learning portal, including tutorials, courses, and book resources.
  - [NumPy User Guide](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/index.html): Suitable for further understanding ndarray, indexing, broadcasting, data types, and numerical computation.
  - [Kaggle Pandas](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/pandas): Primarily short exercises, suitable for quickly familiarizing yourself with common Pandas operations.
  - [Kaggle Data Cleaning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/data-cleaning): Practices Pandas around real data cleaning problems such as missing values, dates, and encoding.
  - [LeetCode 30 Days of Pandas](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/30-days-of-pandas/): Practices filtering, aggregation, merging, and data processing in problem format, suitable for roles that test Pandas in interviews.

- **Machine Learning Algorithms:** When preparing for roles like Data Science, Machine Learning, and AI, you'll need to understand common algorithms and establish a complete machine learning workflow, including data preparation, feature engineering, model selection, training, evaluation, hyperparameter tuning, as well as common issues like overfitting, data leakage, and class imbalance. You can start by building an overall framework, then decide the depth of mastery needed based on your target role. Common resources include:
  - [Machine Learning Specialization — Andrew Ng / DeepLearning.AI](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/machine-learning-introduction): Suitable for machine learning beginners, taught by Andrew Ng, with three courses covering Regression, Classification, and then Neural Networks, Decision Trees, Clustering, Recommender Systems, and Reinforcement Learning. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}
  - [Mathematics for Machine Learning and Data Science — DeepLearning.AI](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/mathematics-for-machine-learning-and-data-science): If your linear algebra, calculus, probability, and statistics foundations are weak, this can help supplement the mathematical background needed for machine learning. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
  - [Deep Learning Specialization — Andrew Ng / DeepLearning.AI](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/deep-learning): Suitable for those who have mastered basic machine learning and wish to further study deep learning content such as Neural Networks, CNN, and Sequence Models. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
  - [IBM Machine Learning Professional Certificate](https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-machine-learning): More practice-oriented, including hands-on labs and projects, suitable for those who want to further familiarize themselves with machine learning applications through projects. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
  - [scikit-learn User Guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/user_guide.html): Scikit-learn's official documentation, which can serve as an index for algorithms and implementations, covering topics like Supervised Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Model Selection, Preprocessing, and Pipelines.
  - [Kaggle Intro to Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intro-to-machine-learning): Suitable for quickly completing your first machine learning project after learning basic concepts.
  - [Kaggle Intermediate Machine Learning](https://www.kaggle.com/learn/intermediate-machine-learning): Further covers practical issues such as Missing Values, Categorical Variables, Pipelines, Cross-validation, XGBoost, and Data Leakage.

- **Git & GitHub:** Git is a common version control tool in development, used to record code changes, manage different versions, and facilitate multi-person collaboration; GitHub is often used for code hosting, collaboration, and publicly showcasing projects. For technical roles like Software Engineering, Data Science, and Machine Learning, a clearly structured GitHub project can also serve as evidence of your abilities. You can focus on the basic Git Workflow, Branch, Merge, Pull Request, README, and project structure. Common resources include:
  - [Pro Git](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2): A complete free e-book provided by the official Git website, offering a systematic introduction to everything from basic commands to Branching and Remote Repositories.
  - [Git Reference](https://git-scm.com/docs): Official Git command documentation, suitable as a daily reference tool.
  - [GitHub Skills](https://skills.github.com/): Official interactive GitHub courses, where you can learn operations like GitHub Flow, Pull Request, Merge Conflict, and GitHub Actions through actual repositories.
  - [GitHub Docs — Get Started](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started): Official GitHub getting started documentation, suitable for understanding basic concepts like Repository, Commit, Branch, and Pull Request.
  - [GitHub Docs — About README](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-readmes): Explains how to write a README. For job application projects, a README can clearly explain the problem the project solves, the data or technologies used, how to run it, the main results, and your contributions.
  - [GitHub Docs — GitHub Flow](https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/using-github/github-flow): Introduces the common collaboration workflow of Branch → Commit → Pull Request → Review → Merge.
  GitHub's green contribution graph itself doesn't directly demonstrate engineering capability. For job applications, a project that runs, is clearly structured, and explains the problem, method, and results clearly through its README, is usually more likely to become evidence of ability that can be discussed in an interview.

## Competitions & Project Evidence

- **Business Competitions:** Suitable for practicing research, business judgment, solution presentation, and teamwork. First, check if the competition theme and deliverables are relevant to your target role before deciding to invest your time. You can read more about [Business Competition Preparation](./business-competition.md).
- **Kaggle:** Suitable for data analysis and machine learning practice. You can also turn public Notebooks, method explanations, and post-mortems into portfolio evidence. The problems and rules are subject to [Kaggle Competitions](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions). You can also check [Kaggle Competition Preparation](./kaggle-competition.md).
- **Course Projects:** You don't necessarily need to start a new large project. Adding problem context, method choices, result validation, and clear explanations to existing course assignments might be more complete than hastily joining new activities.

## Finance & Professional Qualifications

Many finance professionals pursue professional qualifications, so it's easy to feel like "everyone else has one, do I need one too?" CFA, FRM, and HKICPA QP correspond to different knowledge areas, career paths, and qualification requirements, and you don't necessarily have to complete them before starting a job. First, see if the role will utilize them, then decide whether to add them to your job search Path.

### See What These Certifications Specifically Assess

| Path | Closest Content | Common Related Directions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CFA | Investment analysis, valuation, asset classes, portfolio management, and professional ethics | Research, asset management, wealth management, some investment and financing roles |
| FRM | Identification and management of financial risks such as market, credit, operational, and liquidity risks | Risk management, model validation, market and credit risk, risk consulting |
| HKICPA QP | Accounting, financial reporting, business and professional judgment, plus practical experience | Hong Kong auditing, accounting, financial reporting, and related professional services |

The table describes the knowledge focus, but it doesn't mean all related roles require these certifications. Specific JDs, employer training paths, and qualification policies have higher priority.

If you decide to add a certain certification to your job search route, you'll see separate notes about each certification later on. In those, you can sequentially confirm applicable roles, exam structure, registration qualifications, and other information. Exam dates, fees, and policies may change, so always refer to the official pages for the latest information.
You can also choose to only study the modules most needed for the roles you're applying for, and not register for the exam for now. You can then decide whether to complete the professional qualification after you start working.

- For investment and research directions, you can explore **CFA**; for risk directions, you can explore **FRM**; for accounting paths, you can explore **HKICPA QP, CPA, or [ACCA](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/qualifications/glance/acca/overview.html)**.
- For quantitative finance directions, you might also encounter **[CQF](https://www.cqf.com/about-cqf/qualification/overview)**. Unlike short-term problem-solving, this requires a separate evaluation of course content, fees, mathematical foundation, and target role recognition.

### Work Backwards from Recruitment Tasks, Not Forwards from Popularity

When reviewing target roles, you can distinguish between knowledge that will be directly used after joining, test and interview content, explicitly required qualification paths, preferred bonus points, and learning pursued solely out of long-term interest. If the role currently requires SQL, accounting fundamentals, industry research, client communication, etc., supplementing evidence related to these skills might be more direct.
