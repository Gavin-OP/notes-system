# LeetCode

LeetCode isn't just for algorithms; you can also practice SQL directly. To determine if this path is necessary for you, first check the Job Description (JD) for your target role, along with any online tests and interview instructions. While "everyone needs to grind LeetCode for job searching" might sound like a big statement, what truly matters is what the specific role requires.

## First, Understand What the Role Will Test

For data analysis, BI, and some product analysis roles, SQL, data processing, and business problems are more common. Software development and algorithm roles might involve data structures, algorithms, complexity, and live coding. Some roles only require a take-home project or case study and don't primarily focus on LeetCode.

You can confirm the format from the official recruitment website, invitation emails, info sessions, and reliable recent interview feedback. Practicing after clarifying the question types will make your progress more noticeable than aimlessly grinding problems.

## SQL Path

LeetCode's official [SQL 50 Study Plan](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-sql-50/) covers basic to medium-level problems. When practicing, you can progress layer by layer based on your ability:

- SELECT, WHERE, sorting, and conditional expressions;
- Aggregation, GROUP BY, and HAVING;
- INNER / LEFT JOIN and multi-table relationships;
- Subqueries and Common Table Expressions;
- Date, string, and null value handling;
- Window functions, ranking, and moving calculations.

After passing each problem, ask yourself: What is the relationship between the tables? Why did I choose this specific Join? Where do duplicate rows come from? How do null values affect the result? Is there a clearer way to write this? In an interview, explaining your logic is just as important as getting the query to pass.

## Algorithms and Data Structures Path

For software roles, you can start with fundamental patterns like arrays, strings, hash tables, two pointers, stacks and queues, linked lists, binary trees, and graphs. Then, based on your target role, add search algorithms, dynamic programming, or more specialized problem types.

The [Top Interview 150](https://leetcode.com/studyplan/top-interview-150/) is LeetCode's official comprehensive interview plan, but it's more like a map of problem types, and not everyone needs to complete it entirely. It can be more manageable to first identify high-frequency patterns for your target role and your own weak points.

## How to Truly Make a Problem Your Own Skill

You might want to keep these records:

- How you initially understood the problem;
- Why your first idea was feasible or not;
- What pattern you ultimately used;
- The time and space complexity, or the SQL execution logic;
- Common pitfalls like edge cases, null values, or duplicate data;
- Whether you can redo it without looking at the solution after some time.

Understanding a solution is the beginning of comprehension; only by re-deriving it yourself will it gradually become a stable skill. Categorizing incorrect problems by "reading the problem, knowledge, implementation, edge cases, time" can also make it easier to identify your true sticking points.

## Transitioning from Independent Practice to Interview Communication

Practice explaining verbally: First, restate the problem and assumptions, then discuss your basic approach, optimization ideas, complexity, and test cases. While coding, continuously explain key decisions, and if you get stuck, let the interviewer know what you're trying to verify.

In a SQL interview, you can proactively explain table structure, unique keys, and assumptions about NULLs and duplicate records. After completing an algorithm problem, walk through it with normal, edge, and abnormal inputs. Clear communication will make your problem-solving ability feel more complete.

## Establish a Sustainable Rhythm

You can alternate between fundamental problems, reviewing incorrect problems, timed practice, and mock interviews. When you're not feeling your best, reviewing old problems still counts as progress. Continuously doing new problems without reviewing can easily lead to the feeling of "I've seen a lot, but can't recall them during the actual test."

If interview debriefs repeatedly highlight a certain type of gap, adjust your practice focus accordingly. LeetCode is a tool to help you get into your target role; it doesn't need to become a leaderboard for measuring your intelligence.
