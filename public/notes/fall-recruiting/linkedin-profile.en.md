# LinkedIn

LinkedIn is your dynamic professional profile, an ever-unfolding career story. It helps recruiters quickly understand your career direction and delve into your experiences, projects, and activities. Your profile also influences the jobs the platform recommends and how others find you. LinkedIn's algorithm considers your Headline, About section, Experience, Education, and location preferences. A clear profile helps the platform understand your goals, attracting more suitable opportunities.

## Make Your Top Section Instantly Clear

When others visit your profile, the first things they see are your profile picture, Headline, current experience, education background, location, and contact information. This "10-second impression" doesn't need to be crammed with keywords; it just needs to clearly answer: 'Who are you, what are you good at, and what is your target direction?'

**Tips:**
*   **Professional Profile Picture:** Use a clear, professional profile picture that makes you feel confident.
*   **Custom URL:** Change your LinkedIn URL to an easy-to-share custom link (e.g., `linkedin.com/in/yourname`).

Your Headline can follow this structure to make your target direction and core competencies instantly clear:

> Current Role｜Target Direction｜Core Skills or Expertise

For example, instead of just "Master’s Student," consider "Data Science Master’s Student｜Product Analytics｜SQL & Experimentation." If your target isn't fully defined yet, it's perfectly fine to write a broader skill direction; there's no need to pretend to hold a specific position.

## About: An Introduction That Sparks Curiosity and Conversation

The About section can be more conversational than your resume, providing more background information, like an opening statement that leads to further discussion. Here, you can:

*   Explain the problems or job directions you are currently focusing on.
*   Share which experiences have shaped your interests and goals.
*   Highlight your core competencies and working style.
*   Express the teams, projects, or peers you hope to connect with.

Ideally, after reading it, a reader will naturally think of a question to use as an icebreaker when connecting with you. A specific story or scenario is far more memorable than generic adjectives like "passionate, hard-working, team player."

## Experience and Projects: Add Context and Results

For each experience or project, start with one sentence to explain the business or project context. Then, describe in detail the actions you took, the methods/tools you used, and the results you achieved. Compared to a resume, LinkedIn allows you to add more background information, relevant links, and media (such as images, videos, documents) to make your work results more visual. Please ensure that company names, dates, job titles, and achievement data are consistent with your resume.

Student experiences can also be showcased through sections like Projects, Courses, Volunteer Experience, Organizations, and Languages. LinkedIn's official Profile Sections also support Featured, Licenses & Certifications, Publications, and Recommendations; only choose the sections that truly help others understand you.

## Skills: Support Your Abilities with Evidence

A skill list isn't about length, but about persuasiveness. Prioritize skills that are common in target job descriptions and for which you can find specific evidence in your experiences or projects. LinkedIn allows you to associate skills with specific experiences, which is more credible than isolated tags.

You can use these three questions to review your skill list:

*   In which experience have I used this skill? Can I provide specific examples?
*   If an interviewer asks for more, can I clearly explain the process and details?
*   Is this skill still aligned with my current target direction?

## Improve Profile Discoverability and Networking Efficiency

*   **Keyword Optimization:** Naturally integrate keywords common in target job descriptions into your Headline, About, and Experience sections. This helps recruiters and the platform's algorithm find you.
*   **Job Alerts:** Set up Job Alerts, saving several different sets of job keywords and similar job titles to let the system filter opportunities for you.
*   **Build Connections:** When researching target companies or roles on LinkedIn, proactively look for people relevant to your job search direction, such as current employees in target teams, professionals in similar roles, alumni, or individuals with similar majors and career transition experiences. You can start by mentioning shared schools, similar majors, comparable career paths, or common interests. When sending connection invitations, briefly explain your common ground and why you'd like to connect; this usually gets a better response than a lengthy self-introduction.
*   **Stay in Touch:** After connecting, you can gradually learn about roles and teams by arranging Coffee Chats, asking about career experiences, or discussing industry information, and then maintain follow-up contact. After each conversation, note down what you learned. In subsequent interactions, share relevant updates and maintain a genuine relationship.

## Manage Your Privacy Settings and Notifications

You can independently manage the visibility of "Open to Work," your public profile, contact information, and activity notifications. Before making significant updates to your profile, check if you want to broadcast every change to all your connections to avoid unnecessary disturbances. Ensure your profile is discoverable while not revealing information you wish to keep private.

## Perform a Final Check from a Stranger's Perspective

Log out or open your public profile in an incognito window and review it as a recruiter or peer visiting for the first time:

*   **Top Section:** Are your direction and core value immediately clear?
*   **Consistency:** Are dates, companies, and job descriptions consistent with your resume?
*   **Link Validity:** Are all Featured and project links working?
*   **Contact Information:** Is your public email still in active use?
*   **Information Density:** Do older experiences overshadow more relevant, important information?
