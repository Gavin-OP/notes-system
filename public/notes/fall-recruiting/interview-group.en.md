# Group Interview

A group interview isn't about being the loudest voice or rushing to claim a "leader" title. Instead, it's a chance to show how you collaborate effectively under pressure. Recruiters want to see if your group can understand a task, exchange ideas, make collective decisions, and present a clear, coherent outcome together. They're assessing your teamwork, communication, listening skills, problem-solving approach, and how you contribute constructively to a shared goal.

## Understand the Task First

Before anyone jumps to solutions, take a moment to ensure your entire group is aligned on the core objective. You might want to clarify:
*   **What is the specific deliverable?** (e.g., a ranked list, a presentation, a recommendation, a solution to a case study).
*   **What are the key constraints and criteria for success?** (e.g., budget limits, ethical considerations, target audience).
*   **How much time do you have for the entire exercise, and for each stage?**
*   *Try saying:* "Before we dive into ideas, let's quickly confirm what our final output needs to be and how we want to allocate our time. Does that sound like a good starting point for everyone?"

## Roles Emerge, Contributions Matter

You don't need to formally declare a "leader" or fight for a specific role. In a successful group, different contributions naturally emerge and are equally valued. Focus on what helps the group achieve its shared goal. You might find yourself naturally taking on some of these roles or making these contributions:
*   **Contributing original ideas:** Offering new perspectives, data points, or creative solutions.
*   **Structuring the discussion:** Suggesting an agenda, a way to categorize ideas, or a decision-making framework.
*   **Timekeeping:** Gently reminding the group of remaining time or proposing to move to the next stage.
*   **Connecting ideas:** Pointing out similarities or differences between suggestions, helping to build consensus or identify trade-offs.
*   **Inviting participation:** Noticing quieter members and creating space for their input, ensuring diverse perspectives are heard.
*   **Summarising progress:** Periodically recapping what's been discussed or decided to keep everyone aligned and on track.

## Make Your Contributions Count

To make your contributions truly count and advance the discussion, try to:
*   **Build on others' ideas:** Rather than just stating your own point, try to acknowledge what someone else said and then add to it. For example: "I agree with [Name]'s point about X, and I'd like to add a perspective on Y that could impact that."
*   **Disagree constructively:** If you have a different view, explain *why* by referring to the task criteria, assumptions, or potential consequences, rather than dismissing someone's idea. "I see your point about X, but considering our primary objective to Y, perhaps Z option aligns better because..."
*   **Keep the group focused:** If the conversation drifts, gently bring it back to the task. "That's an interesting tangent, but let's make sure we're still addressing [core task element] to stay on schedule."
*   **Invite participation:** Actively create opportunities for others to speak. "We haven't heard from [Name] yet, what are your thoughts on this approach?" or "Does anyone else have a different perspective on this point?"

## Navigating Challenges

Group discussions aren't always smooth sailing. Here's how you might navigate some common situations:
*   **If you're interrupted:** It happens! Don't get flustered. You can briefly pause, then at the next natural opening, politely finish your point or connect back: "To finish my thought on X, I was going to say Y. Now, [interrupter's name], what were you going to add?"
*   **If you're struggling to enter the discussion:** Look for natural pauses, or moments when the group needs a summary or a new direction. "To recap where we are, it seems we've discussed A and B. Perhaps we should now move to C?" or "I'd like to connect [Person 1]'s idea with [Person 2]'s point about X."
*   **When the group chooses a different path:** Once the group makes a decision, even if it wasn't your first choice, support the collective outcome. You can still professionally highlight potential risks or trade-offs if they haven't been fully discussed: "I understand we're moving forward with X. Just to be clear, are we all comfortable accepting the trade-off that Y might happen?"

## Presenting Your Group's Work

The presentation is your group's chance to shine as a unit to the recruiters:
*   **Before presenting:** Quickly confirm who will say what, the order, and the key points or numbers you want to highlight. Ensure everyone is comfortable with the final solution and its rationale.
*   **During Q&A:** Answer questions as a team. Explain the group's collective reasoning, the process you followed, and the trade-offs considered during the discussion. Avoid singling out individuals or blaming others for specific decisions. "Our group decided on X because of Y criteria, and we collectively considered Z as a potential challenge, which led us to this solution."
