# Group Interview

A group interview is not a competition to speak the most or claim the leader title first. It is a compressed collaboration exercise: can the group understand the task, exchange information, make choices, and deliver a result?

## Start with the task

Confirm the deliverable, time, criteria, and constraints. Roles can emerge naturally: contributing evidence, structuring ideas, testing assumptions, watching time, connecting views, or summarising.

## Help the discussion move

- Build on the previous speaker before adding your view.
- Disagree through criteria, not personal judgement.
- Invite quieter participants into the discussion.
- Bring the group back to the task when it drifts.
- Separate agreement from unresolved trade-offs.

If you are interrupted, pause and return at a natural transition. If you have struggled to enter, summarising or connecting two ideas can create a useful opening. Once the group chooses a direction, support the shared answer while making important risks visible.

Before presenting, confirm the structure, timing, speakers, and key evidence. During questions, explain the group’s reasoning rather than blaming one participant for a decision.
